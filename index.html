The problem

Brand rules exist. Nobody sees them while writing.

Content now comes from marketers, agencies, freelancers, sales teams and AI tools. Each reads the guidelines differently, and a PDF cannot stop anyone at the moment they press Publish.

Guidelines live in PDFs

Writers guess. Reviewers repeat the same corrections, and the rules never get clearer.

Facts go stale

Prices, counts and offers change. Old numbers keep appearing in new posts until a customer notices.

Reviewers become the bottleneck

One small team proofreads everything, including content that was never risky.

How it works

From draft to approved in four steps.

Teach it your brand

Admins add guidelines, approved terms, facts with an owner and expiry date, and a register of risky claims.

Paste a draft

Rule checks run live while you type. An optional Gemini deep check catches tone and meaning that keywords miss.

Resolve cited findings

Every flag shows why, the source rule, and a before and after. You accept, edit, or dispute with a reason.

Reviewer approves

Risky items route to a reviewer with comments, version history and an audit trail. Insights show where rules are unclear.

Questions

Things people ask first.

Does the AI approve or rewrite my content?

No. Rule checks run in your browser and suggest a fix. An optional Gemini deep check can add findings, but only a person accepts, edits or disputes them, and only a Reviewer or Admin can approve.

Where does my draft go?

Rule checks stay in your browser. Your draft and rule list are sent to Google Gemini only when you click the deep check. Saved content and its history live in your workspace.

What happens when the AI is wrong?

Every AI finding must cite a rule that exists and quote text that is really in the draft, or it is dropped. The screen tells you how many were dropped, and you can dispute any finding with a reason.

Who can do what?

Creators write and submit. Reviewers approve or request changes but cannot edit drafts. Admins maintain the brand rules, facts and claims.

Does it check images and layouts?

Yes, in part. It reviews text (including text imported from PDF and DOCX files) and one attached PNG or JPG per draft: dominant colors against your palette, alt text, and a text contrast check. Logo recognition and full layout review are not included yet.
